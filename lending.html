02  The difference

A calculator plug-in answers the question it was built for. Your customer asks a different one.

The mortgage widget on a bank site is a form: nine fields, all required, one direction, one product. It is fine at the question it was built for and helpless at every neighboring question — which is usually the one the visitor actually came to ask.

A standard plug-in

What you can ship today
  • A fixed form. Every field required, including the ones your visitor doesn't know yet.
  • One direction. It solves for the payment. "What price fits $2,400 a month?" is a different widget.
  • One product per vendor. A mortgage plug-in, an auto plug-in, a savings plug-in: three integrations, three looks.
  • Somebody else's assumptions. A generic PMI rate, a guessed tax figure, and no idea what your rate sheet says.
  • A number, with no work shown. Nothing for the visitor to understand and nothing for compliance to review.
  • No follow-up. "What if I put ten percent down?" means filling the form out again.

The same page on TrueMath

What this page is demonstrating
  • Plain English in. "I make $12k a month, I've got $650 in other payments and twenty percent down." No form to learn.
  • Any direction. One domain solves for price, payment, down payment, or rate. Same logic, every question.
  • One engine behind all of it. Mortgage, auto, savings and CDs on one integration.
  • Your rules. Your rate sheet, your LTV thresholds, your PMI table, your CD terms — in a domain you own and version.
  • The whole worked answer. Every intermediate step labeled and explained, with the lineage behind each number.
  • A conversation. Follow-ups and what-ifs run as scenarios against the numbers already on the screen.

04  How it fits

The math is a service. The front end is yours.

TrueMath is not a widget you drop on a page. A domain is where the logic lives — variables, units, rules, versions, and an audit trail on every change — and it answers over an API. Your web team builds the interface in your design system, and the same reviewed logic serves the website, the mobile app, the call-center screen, and your loan officers' own tools. One review, one source of truth, every surface.

What your team builds

Your stack, your brand
  • The interface, in your design system and your CMS. Nothing to theme around.
  • As conversational or as form-like as you want — the engine takes plain English or structured input.
  • One integration for every calculator on the site, present and future.
  • A front end that can grow: what-ifs, comparisons, saved scenarios, a shareable result.

What TrueMath handles

The part nobody wants to maintain
  • Deterministic math: the same inputs return the same answer, every time, to the last decimal.
  • The worked steps and the lineage behind every number, ready for a compliance read.
  • A unit system, so a term in years and a term in months are never confused again.
  • Versioning, so a rate-sheet change is a dated revision and not a silent edit.
  • Domains stored with multi-tenant isolation in AWS. TrueMath is SOC 2 ready.
