02  Intake

You send a link. They do the typing.

Pick the calculations you want out of the domain and send the link. Your client opens it at 10pm on their own phone. They fill in the form, or they type what they know into a chat box, or they do some of each. The scenarios come back to you before the first call.

Step 1

Pick the calculations

Choose what this client needs out of the domain. A purchase scenario and a refinance scenario are different picks from the same set, so you are not sending a form full of fields nobody will use.

Step 2

Send the link

Text it or email it. Your client does not install anything, does not make an account, and does not need you on the phone to get through it. The form works on the phone they already have in their hand.

Step 3

Get the scenarios back

Their answers run through the same math you would have run yourself. You open real numbers instead of a voicemail that says call me back.

And once the scenarios are back

Side by side

A link or a PDF

03  Show the work

Every number can show how it got there.

Open a result and the steps behind it are right there. When a client asks where a figure came from, or your processor does, you do not have to rebuild it in your head or go find the spreadsheet you used in March.

What you can see

On any result
  • Every intermediate step, labeled. Not one number at the end of a black box.
  • The values it had to assume. When the math needs a figure your client never gave, it says so and counts them.
  • The inputs that fed each step. You can follow a figure back to where it came from.

Why it stays right

Deterministic, not generated
  • The same inputs give the same result. Every time, to the last decimal.
  • The math is not being written on the fly. It is a reviewed domain, not a model improvising an answer.
  • A rule change is a dated revision. Not a silent edit you find out about later.
If you have ever been asked to defend a number you sent, you know why this matters.
